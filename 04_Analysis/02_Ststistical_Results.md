# Statistical Test Results

## Hypothesis

### Null Hypothesis (H₀)

There is no difference in conversion rates between the Ad and PSA groups.

### Alternative Hypothesis (H₁)

There is a difference in conversion rates between the Ad and PSA groups.

## Statistical Test

A **two-proportion Z-test** was used to compare the conversion rates of the Ad and PSA groups.

Significance level:

**α = 0.05**

## Test Results

| Metric | Result |
|---|---:|
| Z-statistic | 7.3701 |
| P-value | < 0.001 |
| Significance level | 0.05 |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 0.5951% – 0.9434% |

## Statistical Decision

Because the p-value is substantially lower than the significance level of 0.05, the null hypothesis is rejected.

The confidence interval for the conversion-rate difference is entirely above zero, providing additional evidence that the Ad group performed better than the PSA group.

## Statistical Conclusion

The Ad group achieved a **statistically significant higher conversion rate** than the PSA control group.

The observed improvement is therefore unlikely to be explained by random sampling variation alone.

## Effect Size

The Ad group produced:

- **2.5547% conversion rate**

- **0.7692 percentage-point absolute improvement**

- **43.09% relative conversion lift**

Statistical significance indicates that the observed difference is unlikely to be due to chance. It does not, by itself, determine whether the campaign is financially worthwhile.



Business impact, campaign cost, revenue per conversion, and return on investment should be evaluated before unrestricted scaling.
